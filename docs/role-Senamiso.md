### Roles of a Software architect/designer  

Software architecture plays a pivotal role in any software development team by providing 
the foundational blueprint that guides the entire project lifecycle. As defined in Ian 
Sommerville’s Software Engineering (10th ed., Chapter 6), software architecture is the 
fundamental organization of a system, embodied in its components, their relationships, 
and the guiding design principles. In a development team, the software architect (or 
architecture team) is responsible for making early, high-impact design decisions that 
directly influence the system’s performance, security, maintainability, and scalability. 

According to Sommerville, architectural design decisions must address critical 
questions such as: How will the system be structured? What architectural patterns (e.g., 
layered, client-server, or repository) should be used? How will non-functional 
requirements like response time and reliability be satisfied? These decisions are not 
made in isolation; the architect collaborates closely with requirements engineers, 
developers, testers, and stakeholders to ensure the architecture aligns with business 
goals and technical constraints. 

In practice, the architect acts as a bridge between abstract requirements and concrete 
implementation. By documenting multiple architectural views (logical, process, 
development, and physical views, as in Kruchten’s 4+1 model), the architect enables 
clear communication across the team. This reduces misunderstandings and supports 
parallel development—different sub-teams can work on separate components without 
conflicting designs. 

The architect also mitigates risks by evaluating trade-offs early—balancing security 
against performance or scalability against cost. Regular architecture reviews keep the 
team aligned as requirements evolve. By providing this guiding structure, the architect 
empowers the development team to focus on coding and testing rather than constantly 
redesigning core elements.  

Architecture also informs detailed design and implementation. As discussed in Chapter 
7 of Sommerville, this architecture directly informs object-oriented detailed design 
using the UML, the application of design patterns, and decisions on open-source 
component reuse. Without a well-defined architecture, teams risk “big ball of mud” 
systems that are difficult to maintain or scale. Ultimately, the software architect 
ensures the team delivers a coherent, evolvable system that meets both current needs 
and future changes, making architecture the cornerstone of professional software 
development.
